# 09 — Incoming Student Profile: Data-Grounded Portrait

> **Purpose:** Provide an evidence-based portrait of the students who will enter the BS in Enterprise AI (Fall 2026–2030). Every claim is sourced. This document should inform the Student Journey page, syllabi scaffolding, and advising design.

> **Cohort:** Primarily Gen Z (born 1997–2012) with the earliest Gen Alpha (born ~2013+) arriving by 2030. Fall 2026 freshmen were born ~2008; Fall 2030 freshmen were born ~2012.

## 1. Academic Preparation: The NAEP Picture

The National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP) — "The Nation's Report Card" — is the largest nationally representative assessment of U.S. K–12 students. Its 2024 results (tested Jan–Mar 2024, released 2025) paint a challenging picture of the pipeline feeding into higher education.

### 1.1 Reading / Literacy

| Grade | Year | At/Above Proficient | Below Basic | Source |
|-------|------|-------------------|-------------|--------|
| 4 | 2019 | 35% | 34% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 4 | 2022 | 33% | 37% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 4 | 2024 | 31% | ~40% (record) | NAGB press release, Jan 2025 |
| 8 | 2019 | 34% | 27% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 8 | 2022 | 31% | 30% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 8 | 2024 | ~30% | ~33% (record) | NAGB press release, Jan 2025 |
| 12 | 2019 | 37% | 30% | NAGB, Sep 2025 |
| 12 | 2024 | 35% | 32% (record) | NAGB, Sep 2025 |

**Key findings:**
- Average reading scores **fell at every tested grade** from 2019 to 2024. No state posted reading gains at grades 4 or 8 in 2024. (NAGB, Jan 2025)
- Grade 12 reading in 2024 was the **lowest 12th-grade average on record**. (NAGB, Sep 2025)
- The share of students "below NAEP Basic" — meaning they cannot demonstrate even partial mastery of grade-level material — hit **record highs** at grades 4, 8, and 12 in 2024.
- Long-Term Trend (LTT): Age 13 reading fell 4 points vs 2020; only **14% of 13-year-olds reported reading for fun "almost every day"** — the lowest on record. (NAEP LTT, nationsreportcard.gov)
- Declines began **pre-pandemic** (mid-2010s) and accelerated through COVID disruptions. The sharpest drops concentrated among lower-performing students. (NAGB)

**What this means for BSEAI:** ~65–69% of incoming freshmen demonstrated **less than proficient** reading ability in 8th grade. By 12th grade, ~65% are below proficient. Students will need explicit scaffolding for technical reading, documentation literacy, and sustained analytical writing. The studio model's emphasis on "translation briefs" and documentation-as-artifact directly addresses this gap.

### 1.2 Mathematics

| Grade | Year | At/Above Proficient | Below Basic | Source |
|-------|------|-------------------|-------------|--------|
| 4 | 2019 | 41% | 19% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 4 | 2022 | 36% | 25% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 4 | 2024 | 39% | 24% | NAEP 2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 8 | 2019 | 34% | 31% | NAEP 2022/2024, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 8 | 2022 | 26% | 38% | NAEP 2022, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 8 | 2024 | ~27% (not sig. different from '22) | ~37% | NCES, nationsreportcard.gov |
| 12 | 2019 | 24% | 40% | NAGB, Sep 2025 |
| 12 | 2024 | lower than 2019 | higher than 2019 | NAGB, Sep 2025 |

**Key findings:**
- Grade 4 math showed a **2-point recovery** from 2022 to 2024 (the only bright spot), but remains below 2019 levels and far below the 2013 peak.
- Grade 8 math showed **no significant recovery** from the historic 2022 drop. Only ~27% of 8th graders are proficient — meaning **~73% are not proficient in 8th-grade math**.
- Grade 12 math scores in 2024 were **lower than 2019**. (NAGB, Sep 2025)
- The 2022 math results represented the **largest score decline in NAEP math history** at grade 8 — a 8-point drop from 2019.
- OECD PISA 2022: The U.S. national math average **remained behind many other industrialized nations**; 15-year-old students in Singapore, Macau, Hong Kong, Taiwan, South Korea, and Japan were largely unaffected or even improved. Math and reading skills globally were in "unprecedented decline." (Reuters, Dec 2023; OECD PISA 2022)

**What this means for BSEAI:** Most NJIT freshmen will have completed high school during the worst math-performance era in NAEP history. The program should not assume algebraic fluency or comfort with quantitative reasoning. IS 117's computational thinking strand and IS 331's data literacy scaffolding must account for this reality.

### 1.3 Long-Term Trend: The Decline Is Not Just COVID

NAEP data shows a decade of decline that **predates the pandemic**:
- Reading scores at all grades were already trending down from their mid-2010s peaks before COVID hit.
- Math at grade 8 was flat from 2009–2019 and then collapsed in 2022.
- The pandemic **deepened** existing trends but did not create them. (NAGB)
- Globally, the OECD PISA 2022 results confirmed a "continuation of a long-term decline in reading and mathematical skills since the early 2010s." COVID was "only one contributing factor." (Reuters, Dec 2023)

## 2. College Readiness: ACT Data

The ACT test measures college readiness against established benchmarks (the score at which a student has ~50% chance of earning a B or higher in a first-year college course).

### ACT Benchmarks
| Subject | Benchmark Score |
|---------|----------------|
| English | 18 |
| Reading | 22 |
| Math | 22 |
| Science | 23 |
| STEM (Math + Science) | 26 |

### Class of 2024 National Results (released Oct 2024)

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Average composite score | **19.4** | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met English benchmark | 51% | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met Reading benchmark | 40% | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met Math benchmark | **29%** | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met Science benchmark | 30% | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met ALL four benchmarks | **20%** | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Met zero benchmarks | **43%** | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |
| Students tested | 1,374,791 (~36% of graduates) | ACT Inc., Oct 2024 |

**Key findings:**
- Only **1 in 5** graduates who took the ACT met all four college readiness benchmarks.
- **43%** — nearly half — met **none** of the benchmarks.
- Math readiness (29%) is the weakest area, consistent with NAEP trends.
- The average composite of 19.4 represents a multi-year decline; pre-pandemic averages were ~21.

**What this means for BSEAI:** Even among the self-selected population that takes the ACT, fewer than 1 in 3 are math-ready for college. NJIT admits a higher-performing slice, but the national pipeline is weakening. First-year courses must be designed to build, not assume, foundational quantitative skills.

## 3. College Remediation Rates

National data on developmental/remedial education provides another lens on incoming preparation:

- **~40% of entering college students** require at least one developmental (remedial) course. (Complete College America, 2024; National Center for Education Statistics)
- At open-access institutions (community colleges, some state universities), remediation rates exceed **60%** in math and **30%** in English. (NCES, 2023)
- Students in developmental courses are significantly **less likely to complete a degree**: only ~25% of students who start in developmental math earn a degree within 6 years, versus ~50% for those who start in college-level courses. (Complete College America)
- The COVID-era learning loss has been projected to **increase remediation demand** through at least 2028 as pandemic-affected cohorts reach college age. (Inside Higher Ed, 2023)

**What this means for BSEAI:** The BSEAI studio spine IS 117→118 effectively functions as embedded preparation — building computational confidence, reading habits, and technical writing from scratch rather than assuming them. This is not remediation disguised; it's an instructional design reality.

## 4. The Gen Z Student: Who They Are (Data-Grounded)

Fall 2026 freshmen are primarily late Gen Z (born ~2007–2008). The following characterization uses peer-reviewed research, government data, and major survey findings.

### 4.1 Digital Natives, Not Digitally Literate

The "digital native" label obscures a critical distinction: **fluent consumer ≠ capable producer**.

- The 2018 International Computer and Information Literacy Study (ICILS), testing 42,000 eighth-graders in 14 countries, found **only 2%** were "sufficiently proficient with information devices" to justify the "digital native" label, and **only 19%** could work independently with computers to gather and manage information. (Strauss, Washington Post, Nov 2019; ICILS 2018)
- By the early 2020s, many Gen Z workers entered the workplace **without basic ICT skills** including touch typing. (Demopoulos, The Guardian, Feb 2023; Wells, WSJ, Aug 2024)
- Oxford English Dictionary chose **"brain rot"** as its 2024 word of the year, recognizing the internet culture consuming Gen Z and Alpha. (Oxford Dictionaries, 2024)

**Implication:** IS 117 must teach filesystem navigation, terminal use, and Git from zero. The "digital native" assumption is empirically false for the skills that matter in software development.

### 4.2 Screen Time and Attention

- 41% of Gen Z spend **3+ hours/day** on computers for non-school purposes (vs. 22% in 2004). (Sparks and Honey, 2014)
- OECD found that time spent on electronic devices increased from **<2 hours/weekday in 2012 to ~3 hours in 2019**, displacing extracurricular reading. (OECD PISA, 2019; Reuters, Dec 2019)
- 77% of Gen Z respondents (ages 18–24) agreed: **"When nothing is occupying my attention, the first thing I do is reach for my phone."** (Microsoft, 2015)
- A 2019 meta-analysis of studies from ~20 countries found **watching TV negatively correlated with school grades, language fluency, and math ability**; excessive internet use negatively correlated with grades as well. The effects of screen time were **stronger among adolescents than children**. (Adelantado-Renau et al., JAMA Pediatrics, Sep 2019)
- OECD PISA 2019 found reading scores **declined more** among students spending more time on devices, even though they read more on screens. (OECD, 2019)

**Implication:** The studio model's project-based structure with physical artifacts (shipped websites, deployed apps, portfolio pieces) counteracts passive consumption. Weekly deliverables create structure for students whose default mode is scroll, not build.

### 4.3 Reading Habits

- Gen Z spends **more time on electronic devices and less time reading books** than any prior generation measured by OECD. (OECD, 2019; Sliwa, APA, Aug 2018)
- Only **14% of 13-year-olds** read for fun "almost every day" — the lowest on record. (NAEP LTT, 2023)
- Vocabulary usage among schoolchildren has **markedly declined**, with implications for academic performance. (N. Nicholson, NZ research; Busby, The Independent, Apr 2018)
- However, BookTok (TikTok reading community) has stimulated a partial **revival of volitional reading** among Gen Z, especially among teenage girls. (Mathis, The Week, Feb 2024; Flood, The Guardian, Jun 2021)
- Gen Z readers prefer **fan fiction, manga, escapism, and romantic fantasy** over traditional literary forms. (Roy, FT, May 2025; various)

**Implication:** Course readings must be short, purposeful, and immediately applied. Long unstructured reading assignments will fail. The "inquiry" model — read a 3-page article, produce a 1-page analysis — respects these preferences while building the skill.

### 4.4 Mental Health

The mental health crisis among Gen Z is **well-documented and severe**:

- **46% of American Gen Z** have been diagnosed with a mental health condition. (Harmony Healthcare IT, Jun 2025)
- **37% of U.S. high school students** reported regular mental health struggles during the COVID-19 pandemic. (CDC, cited by Pew Research, Apr 2022)
- Depression among American teenagers has been **rising steadily**, with teen girls particularly affected. (Pew Research, Jul 2019; CDC data)
- Sleep deprivation is widespread: Canadian teens average **6.5–7.5 hours/night**, well below the recommended 10 hours. Sleep loss impairs mood, emotional regulation, and cognitive function. (Various, compiled in Wikipedia Gen Z article; University of Rochester, Jan 2020)
- Gen Z has been described as **"a more educated, well-behaved, stressed, and depressed generation"** compared to predecessors. (The Economist, Feb 2019)
- The most common psychiatric disorders: **ADHD, anxiety disorders, behavioral disorders, and depression**. (Meta-analyses: 2015, 2020; WHO and PISA data)
- Up to **10% of the population** has specific learning disabilities (dyscalculia, dyslexia, ADHD, autism spectrum). (UCL, Apr 2013)

**Implication:** The Human Edge capability of **Resilience Thinking** (IS 219) directly addresses this. The Failure Mode Analysis and Incident Drill signature assessment normalizes failure as data. Studio cohort structure provides built-in community. Advisors must be prepared for mental health accommodations as routine, not exceptional.

### 4.5 Resilience and Failure

- Gen Z **proved less resilient than older cohorts** during COVID disruptions, though their fundamental values did not change. (Harari et al., Current Psychology, Aug 2022)
- They crave **security** in what they perceive as a "volatile, precarious, complex, and ambiguous" world. (Wright, Psychology Today, Jan 2025; various)
- Many Generation Z find themselves having to navigate **economic stagnation** in Europe and housing crises in the Anglosphere, which contributes to demoralization. (Burn-Murdoch, FT, Jul 2025; Nov 2025)
- At the same time, Gen Z values **ambition, creativity, and curiosity** more than the general population. (NielsenIQ, Aug 2023)

**Implication:** The program must simultaneously build resilience (which is underdeveloped) and channel ambition (which is present). The "shipped artifact every semester" model gives concrete proof of capability, counteracting the earned helplessness that many students carry from their K–12 experience.

### 4.6 Work Preferences and Values

- Most important career factors: **skill development** (24%) and **income** (23%). Least: fame (3%) and organizational impact (13%). (Varkey Foundation, Jan 2017)
- Gen Z prioritizes **financial security, meaning, and work-life balance**. (Deloitte Gen Z Survey, 2025)
- They are skeptical consumers who **shun firms whose actions and values are contradictory**. (Various surveys)
- In the U.S., youth unemployment (16-24) was **7.5% in May 2023** — the lowest in 70 years. However, CS new-grad unemployment was **6.1%** (NY Fed, Dec 2024), suggesting tech is an exception to the broader strength.

**Implication:** The program's framing as "The Human Edge" — skills AI cannot replicate, leading to durable career value — directly speaks to Gen Z's desire for both financial security and meaning. The salary data ($110K–$150K entry-level) in the program materials provides the financial motivation; the Human Edge capabilities provide the meaning.

## 5. Gen Alpha Preview (Students Arriving ~2029–2030)

Generation Alpha (born ~2010–2025, per McCrindle) will begin entering college by 2028–2030. Early data is limited but suggestive:

- Born entirely in the **smartphone/iPad era**. Many received devices before age 2. (McCrindle, Jan 2025)
- Their K–12 experience has been **more disrupted** than any prior generation: pandemic school closures during formative elementary years (ages 6–10 for the oldest Alphas during 2020–2021).
- NAEP 2024 data for 4th graders (born ~2014–2015 = Gen Alpha) shows **40% below Basic in reading** — the highest share since 2002 and the worst cohort to enter the pipeline.
- The OECD PISA "unprecedented decline" in math and reading applies globally, not just the U.S.
- Gen Alpha is growing up with **generative AI as a baseline assumption**, not a novelty. ChatGPT launched when the oldest Alphas were ~12. By the time they reach college, AI tools will be fully normalized.
- Mobile phone bans in schools are spreading globally (2024+), suggesting institutional recognition that device management is failing. (Stechyson & Fraser, CBC News, Aug 2024)

**Implication:** Gen Alpha freshmen (entering ~2029–2030) may arrive with even weaker foundational reading and math than current cohorts, but with more intuitive AI-tool fluency. The program's design — which leverages AI tools while developing the human skills that make AI use valuable — is well-positioned for this transition.

## 6. The NJIT Context

NJIT draws primarily from the New Jersey / New York metro area:

- **NJIT is a public research university** with moderately selective admissions. Incoming freshmen are above the national median but still subject to the trends documented above.
- New Jersey's NAEP scores in 2024: slightly above the national average (NP 237 for grade 4 math; NJ typically scores higher), but still showing the same decline patterns.
- NJIT's student body is **highly diverse**: no ethnic majority, significant first-generation and commuter populations.
- Many NJIT students work while studying, adding time pressure that compounds the academic preparation gaps documented above.

## 8. Design Implications for the BSEAI Program

The data above implies several non-negotiable design principles:

### 8.1 Start from Zero — Empirically Justified
The IS 117 → IS 118 sequence must assume **no prior terminal, Git, or programming experience** and **below-proficient reading and math skills**. This is not pessimism — it's what the national data requires.

### 8.2 Build Reading Through Doing
Long reading assignments will fail. Short, purposeful readings immediately applied to builds work. The "inquiry" model — read 3 pages, produce 1 page — respects measured attention and reading levels while building the skill.

### 8.3 Make Failure Safe Before Making It Required
The Resilience Thinking capability (IS 219) comes in Year 3, but failure tolerance must be **scaffolded from Week 1**. Given mental health data, early courses need clear rubrics, multiple revision opportunities, and "failure as data" framing.

### 8.4 Physical Artifacts Over Abstract Knowledge
A generation shaped by passive screen consumption needs to **produce**, not just consume. The shipped-artifact-every-semester model is pedagogically correct for this cohort. The portfolio is the antidote to "I spent four years in college and have nothing to show."

### 8.5 Community by Design
Gen Z reports increasing social isolation despite constant connectivity. The studio cohort model (same peers across 8 courses) provides **forced community** — the thing they need but won't create on their own.

### 8.6 Financial Motivation Is Real and Valid
The salary data ($110K–$150K entry-level) is a legitimate enrollment driver for students who watched their parents struggle through inflation and housing crises. The program should present career outcomes honestly and prominently — this cohort responds to pragmatism, not idealism alone.

### 8.7 AI Fluency Cannot Substitute for Foundational Skills
Students may arrive with ChatGPT fluency but without the ability to evaluate AI output. The AI Audit Log (IS 218) and epistemic humility framing address this head-on: you cannot judge AI output without the foundational knowledge to recognize when it's wrong.
