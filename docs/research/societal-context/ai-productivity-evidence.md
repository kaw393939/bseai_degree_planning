## Answer

Here’s the short answer, as of February 22, 2026: credible studies bracket the near- to medium‑term boost to aggregate labor productivity from generative AI at roughly 0.1 to 1.5 percentage points per year, with wide uncertainty and slow ramp-up tied to adoption, reorganization, and complementary capital.

Key estimates (levels and annual growth effects)
- McKinsey (global): GenAI alone could add 0.1–0.6 pp to annual labor-productivity growth through 2040; combining GenAI with broader automation could lift productivity 0.5–3.4 pp per year, conditional on redeploying time saved. ([mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier?utm_source=openai))
- Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research (global/US): With widespread adoption, AI could raise global annual labor‑productivity growth by about 1.4–1.5 pp for a decade, implying roughly a 7% higher level of global GDP after 10 years; their US profile rises from a 0.1 pp GDP boost in 2027 to 0.4 pp by 2034. ([gspublishing.com](https://www.gspublishing.com/content/research/en/reports/2023/03/27/d64e052b-0f6e-45d7-967b-d7be35fabd16.html?utm_source=openai))
- OECD (G7 focus; meta‑view): Across studies, predicted annual labor‑productivity gains over the next decade span 0.5–3.5 pp; OECD’s own micro‑to‑macro modeling suggests 0.2–1.3 pp for the G7, varying by country. ([oecd.org](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-compendium-of-productivity-indicators-2025_b024d9e1-en/full-report/productivity-in-a-shifting-geopolitical-and-economic-landscape_7b0e7702.html?utm_source=openai))
- IMF (Europe): Medium‑term aggregate gains look modest in baseline simulations—about 1% higher TFP cumulatively over five years (≈0.2 pp per year), with sizable cross‑country dispersion and potential drag from regulation. ([imf.org](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-and-productivity-in-europe-565924?utm_source=openai))
- Penn Wharton Budget Model (US, macro model with adoption curve): AI raises the level of TFP/GDP by ~1.5% by 2035 and ~3% by 2055; the peak addition to annual productivity growth is about 0.2 pp in the early 2030s, fading thereafter (a small persistent 0.04 pp remains from sectoral reweighting). ([budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/issues/2025/9/8/projected-impact-of-generative-ai-on-future-productivity-growth?utm_source=openai))

What micro/field evidence shows (task- and firm-level)
- Writing/analysis tasks (RCT): Access to ChatGPT cut completion time ~40% and raised measured quality ~18% among professionals. Big gains accrue to lower‑baseline performers. ([news.mit.edu](https://news.mit.edu/2023/study-finds-chatgpt-boosts-worker-productivity-writing-0714?utm_source=openai))
- Large-scale deployment in customer support (quasi‑experiment at a Fortune 500 firm): +14% average productivity; +30–35% for the least‑experienced agents; evidence that AI disseminates expert “best practices.” ([gsb.stanford.edu](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/working-papers/generative-ai-work?utm_source=openai))
- Online retail field experiments (multiple GenAI features, millions of users): 0–16% sales lift depending on workflow; with inputs/prices fixed, effects map to TFP improvements; larger gains for smaller/newer sellers. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12049?utm_source=openai))

How to read these numbers for 2025–2026
- Near‑term macro impact is building but not yet uniform in national statistics; most models put the biggest annual boost in the late 2020s to early 2030s as adoption, process redesign, and capital deepening (e.g., data centers, tooling) catch up. ([oecd.org](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-compendium-of-productivity-indicators-2025_b024d9e1-en/full-report/insights-on-productivity-developments-in-2024_c4061fb7.html?utm_source=openai))
- Upper‑bound figures (≈1.5 pp/yr for a decade) assume broad, rapid diffusion and effective reorganization; central estimates cluster lower (≈0.1–0.7 pp/yr) and stress that time saved must be reallocated to value‑adding work to show up in GDP. ([mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier?utm_source=openai))
