## Answer

Here’s a concise, up-to-date readout on where things stand and where they’re headed.

Where debt stands (late 2025–early 2026)
- Gross federal debt (total public debt outstanding): $37.6T at FY2025 year‑end (Sep 30, 2025), $38.5T by Dec 31, 2025, and on pace to reach about $39T by early March 2026. ([gao.gov](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107908?utm_source=openai))
- Debt held by the public (the metric used for debt‑to‑GDP): about 100% of GDP “today” in CBO’s latest baseline (Feb 2026). Gross debt is higher (roughly the low‑120s percent of GDP in late 2025). ([crfb.org](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook?utm_source=openai))

Debt-to-GDP ratio
- End of FY2024 baseline: debt held by the public ~98% of GDP (Treasury’s Financial Report). CBO projects it rising to 108% by 2030 and 120% by 2036 under current law. ([fiscal.treasury.gov](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/financial-report/government-financial-position-and-condition.html?utm_source=openai))

Interest costs and their share of the budget
- FY2025 net interest (budget concept): roughly $0.95–$1.0T, about 14% of total federal outlays; it was the third‑largest line item after Social Security and Medicare. CBO/BPC expect net interest to exceed $1T in FY2026 and roughly double to ~$2.1T by 2036. Note: On a financial‑accounting basis that includes intragovernmental accruals, Treasury’s interest cost for FY2025 was about $1.2T. ([americanactionforum.org](https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/highlights-of-cbos-january-2025-budget-and-economic-outlook/?utm_source=openai))

Deficits
- FY2025 deficit was about $1.8–$1.9T (~6% of GDP). CBO’s new 10‑year outlook (Feb 2026) shows annual deficits remaining above 5% of GDP and totaling about $24.4T over 2026–2035. ([americanactionforum.org](https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/highlights-of-cbos-january-2025-budget-and-economic-outlook/?utm_source=openai))

CBO projections (Feb 2026 baseline)
- Debt held by the public: ~100% of GDP now → 108% by 2030 → 120% by 2036.
- Net interest: ~3.2% of GDP in 2025 → ~4.6% by 2036 (~$2.1T nominal).
- Bottom line: spending continues to outpace revenues under current law; trust funds (e.g., Social Security Old‑Age) face depletion in the early 2030s absent changes. ([crfb.org](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook?utm_source=openai))

Is this “unsustainable”? What would a crisis look like?
- Both CBO and Treasury’s Financial Report characterize the long‑term path as unsustainable because debt rises faster than the economy under current policies. That increases the risk of slower growth, higher rates, and eventually loss of fiscal space. ([crfb.org](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook?utm_source=openai))
- A sudden fiscal crisis is not the base case for a country with deep capital markets and its own currency, but risks rise with:
  - Persistent primary (non‑interest) deficits,
  - Interest rates staying above growth for long periods,
  - Large, rapid rollover needs at higher rates, or
  - Self‑inflicted shocks (e.g., debt‑limit brinkmanship). ([crfb.org](https://www.crfb.org/papers/analysis-cbos-march-2025-long-term-budget-outlook?utm_source=openai))

Quick takeaways for 2025–2026
- The $36T mark is in the rear‑view; gross debt is about $38½T (Dec 2025) and likely near $39T by early March 2026. ([economy.com](https://www.economy.com/united-states/outstanding-public-debt/?utm_source=openai))
- Debt‑to‑GDP (public debt) is roughly 100% now and rising on current policy. ([crfb.org](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook?utm_source=openai))
- Net interest already rivals or exceeds most major program areas and is set to grow the fastest over the next decade. ([bipartisanpolicy.org](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/the-fiscal-outlook-in-cbos-latest-10-year-baseline/?utm_source=openai))
