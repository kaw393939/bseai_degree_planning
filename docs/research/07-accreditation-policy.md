## 1. ABET Computing Accreditation Criteria (2025–2026 Cycle)

**Source:** [abet.org](https://abet.org) — Computing Accreditation Commission (CAC)

### General Criteria (all baccalaureate computing programs)

**5 Required Student Outcomes:**
1. Analyze complex computing problems using principles of computing, mathematics, and relevant domains
2. Design, implement, and evaluate a computing-based solution
3. Communicate effectively in a variety of professional contexts
4. Recognize professional responsibilities and make informed judgments in computing practice (ethics, legal, security, social)
5. Function effectively as a member or leader of a team

**Curriculum minimums:**
- Appropriate mathematics, statistics, and science
- **≥30 semester credit hours (SCH)** of current computing topics
- Continuous improvement via student outcome assessment

### Most Likely ABET Classification: Information Systems

**IS Program Criteria:**
- ≥30 SCH in IS (application development, programming, data management/DB, IT infrastructure, systems analysis/design, project management, and the role of IS in organizations)
- ≥15 SCH in IS environment (business/domain context)
- A major project
- Appropriate math/stat modeling

**Alternative: Information Technology**
- ≥45 SCH IT with fundamentals (info management, integrated systems, platforms, networking, software dev/mgmt, web/mobile)
- Advanced topics, experiential learning, IT project management
- ≥6 SCH math including discrete

**Key note:** No specific "AI" ABET program criteria exist yet. The BS in Enterprise AI must map to either IS or IT criteria. IS is the most natural fit given the existing departmental home (Informatics).

### Data Science (for reference if dual-classification considered)

- ≥45 SCH covering DS lifecycle, ethics/fairness/governance, applied stats/math/computing, an application area, major project

## 2. MSCHE (Middle States) — New Degree Program Requirements

**Source:** [msche.org](https://msche.org), official policy documents

### When Prior MSCHE Approval IS Required

| Trigger | Action Needed |
|---------|--------------|
| **Significant departure** — program "significantly different from and not a logical extension" of existing offerings | Substantive change filing for Significant Departure before implementation |
| **New/higher credential level** — first program at a higher credential level | MSCHE review required |
| **New delivery method** — first-time distance education | Substantive change for Alternative Delivery Method |
| **Third-party providers** — 25–49% domestic or >25% international | Notification or prior approval |

### When Prior Approval May NOT Be Needed

- Programs at **lower** credential levels than already in scope
- **Not a significant departure AND within already-approved credential level**

### Critical Question for NJIT

If NJIT already offers bachelor's degrees in IS/IT/CS, a new BS in Enterprise AI at the same credential level that is deemed a **"logical extension"** of existing computing programs **may not require MSCHE prior approval**. This determination is critical and must be confirmed with the provost's office and MSCHE liaison.

### Submission Deadlines (6 windows per year)

| Submit By | Review In |
|-----------|-----------|
| Jan 2 | February |
| Mar 1 | April |
| May 1 | June |
| Jul 1 | August |
| Sep 1 | October |
| Nov 1 | December |

Submit ≥3 months before intended start date.

### Required Documentation

Must demonstrate compliance with:
- **Standard III:** Design and delivery; rigor, credit hours, coherence
- **Standard V:** Assessment of student learning
- **Standard VI:** Planning, resources, institutional improvement
- Plus Requirements of Affiliation (authorization/licensure)

**Submission contents:** Curriculum, learning outcomes + assessment plan, qualified faculty, student support, library/IT, budget/resources, governance/approval history, teach-out plans.

## 3. NJIT Syllabus Requirements

**Source:** NJIT Office of Digital Learning — Best Practices in Syllabus Design

### Required Elements (every course must include)

1. Course name/number and **mode of instruction** (6 official modes: Face-to-Face, Converged Learning, Synchronous Online, Online, Hybrid, HyFlex)
2. Instructor info including responsiveness expectations (e.g., 24-hr email turnaround)
3. Course description and prerequisites/co-requisites
4. **Course Learning Outcomes** using measurable verbs per Bloom's Taxonomy: "By the end of the course, students will be able to..."
5. Required materials: title, author, edition, publisher, ISBN
6. **Grading policy:** weights, numerical-to-letter scale, NJIT grading policy, final exam weights
7. Feedback policy for major assessments
8. Assessment categories with descriptions
9. Exam information (dates, make-up, proctoring)
10. Late work policy
11. **Academic integrity statement** (required verbatim text)
12. Class etiquette expectations (refs NJIT Code of Student Conduct)
13. Weekly expectations (differentiated for F2F vs online)
14. Week-by-week schedule (topics, readings, tasks, due dates)
15. **Accommodations statement** (refs Office of Accessibility Resources and Services)
16. Canvas accessibility statement
17. NJIT services/technical support link

### Design Philosophy

- "Backward planning" — start with desired outcomes, work backward to teaching techniques, texts, and assignments
- Assignment-centered rather than content-centered
- Emphasizes metacognition, challenging misconceptions, connecting material to experience
- AI detection tools are unreliable; rely on design, process evidence, and oral checks rather than punitive detectors
