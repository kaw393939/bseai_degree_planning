## Answer

Here are the headline estimates from the sources you named (what share of work/tasks could be automated, likely displacement, and timing). I’ve included the publication dates so you can see what’s most current.

McKinsey (McKinsey Global Institute)
- By 2030, automation (accelerated by generative AI) could cover about 30% of current hours worked in the United States and 27% in Europe; even without gen AI, about 20% of hours could be automated. McKinsey projects roughly 12 million occupational transitions in the U.S. by 2030 in a faster‑adoption case. Published May 21, 2024. ([mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/a-new-future-of-work-the-race-to-deploy-ai-and-raise-skills-in-europe-and-beyond?utm_source=openai))
- Across the economy, the technical potential for current technologies (incl. gen AI) to automate work activities has risen to 60–70% of employee time; about half of today’s work activities could be automated sometime between 2030 and 2060 (midpoint ~2045). 2023–2024 synthesis. ([mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-AI-the-next-productivity-frontier?utm_source=openai))

Goldman Sachs (Global Investment Research)
- Ultimately, ~25% of labor tasks in advanced economies and 10–20% in emerging economies could be automated; Goldman expects a measurable boost to U.S. GDP starting in 2027, contributing about 0.4 percentage points to annual U.S. growth by 2034 in their baseline. Nov 7, 2023 (updated explainer on GS site). ([goldmansachs.com](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/ai-may-start-to-boost-us-gdp-in-2027?utm_source=openai))
- Exposure is broad: roughly two‑thirds of U.S. jobs are exposed to some degree of AI automation, though most are partially exposed rather than fully replaceable. 2024–2025 GS explainers referencing GIR. ([goldmansachs.com](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-us-labor-market-is-automating-and-more-flex?utm_source=openai))
- Displacement scale and timing: GS’s 2025 read is that 6–7% of U.S. employment could be displaced over the next decade under wide adoption, with a roughly 0.5 percentage‑point, temporary bump in the unemployment rate during the transition. Aug 13, 2025. ([goldmansachs.com](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/how-will-ai-affect-the-global-workforce?utm_source=openai))

IMF
- Global exposure: about 40% of employment worldwide is exposed to AI; in advanced economies, about 60% of jobs are exposed. Roughly half of exposed jobs are likely to be complemented (productivity gains), and half face substitution risk (lower labor demand/wages, some job loss). Staff Discussion Note and accompanying blog, Jan 14–15, 2024. ([imf.org](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Staff-Discussion-Notes/Issues/2024/01/14/Gen-AI-Artificial-Intelligence-and-the-Future-of-Work-542379?utm_source=openai))
- 2026 update/remarks: At the Jan 2026 World Economic Forum, IMF’s managing director reiterated that about 60% of jobs in advanced economies and about 40% globally will be affected, warning that younger workers could face outsized disruption as entry‑level roles change or disappear. Jan 23, 2026. ([theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jan/23/ai-tsunami-labour-market-youth-employment-says-head-of-imf-davos?utm_source=openai))

How to read these numbers
- “Hours/tasks that could be automated” (McKinsey, GS) is not the same as “jobs eliminated.” It signals how much of current work content can be done by today’s or near‑term tech; net job outcomes depend on adoption speed, redeployment, demand growth, and policy. ([mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/The-economic-potential-of-generative-AI-The-next-productivity-frontier?utm_source=openai))
- “Exposure” (IMF, GS) means jobs where AI can affect tasks and skills; some will be complemented (higher productivity/wages), others substituted (lower demand), with distribution differing by country and occupation mix. ([imf.org](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity?utm_source=openai))
