## Issue 1: GPT-5.3 Codex Claim Needs Verification (HIGH)

"OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex 'was instrumental in creating itself' (OpenAI, February 5, 2026)"

This is a specific, attributed claim with a date. If this can't be independently verified, or if the quote is paraphrased inaccurately, a critic will use it to undermine the entire page. The quotation marks imply a direct quote.

**Fix:** Verify the exact source. If it's from a blog post or press release, add a URL. If it's approximate, change to reported speech without quotation marks.

---

## Issue 2: METR Projection Extrapolations Are Bold (MEDIUM)

The page projects:
- Late 2026: AI handles full-workday tasks
- Mid 2027: Multi-day projects
- Early 2028: Week-long sprints
- 2029: Month-long projects

These are reasonable extrapolations from METR data, but the hedge "if this trajectory holds" may not be enough for an academic audience. If the doubling rate flattens (which is possible), these become easy ammunition.

**Fix:** Strengthen the caveat: "If the current trajectory holds — and note that the doubling time has only accelerated so far — the projections suggest..." Consider adding a brief note acknowledging that historically, exponentials don't hold forever.

## Issue 3: METR Date Ranges Slightly Different From Why-Now Page (LOW)

This page says "2019–2025: ~7 months" and "2023–2026: ~4.3 months."
`why-now.md` says "2019–March 2025: ~196 days (~7 months)" and "Since 2023: 130.8 days."

The precision differs but the numbers are consistent. Not a real issue, but a pedantic reader could note the different date anchors.

**Fix:** Use identical date ranges across both pages for bulletproof consistency.
