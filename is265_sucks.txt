IS 265 - 001: Introduction to Information
Systems (Enterprise AI Edition)

Course Description
Introduce how modern organizations design, operate, and evolve enterprise information
systems, and how AI (especially generative AI) is incorporated responsibly into processes,
products, and decision-making—at the level of architecture, governance, and implementation
practices.

Course Objectives
The course intends to build a wide range of analytical, communication, interpersonal, leadership,
and technology skills. Students will be able to:
 Explain IS through an enterprise architecture lens (strategy, process, application, data,
infrastructure).
 Map a business process and propose improvements using automation + analytics + AI
(including GenAI use cases).
 Describe core enterprise system categories (ERP/CRM/SCM, data platforms, workflow
systems) and how they integrate.
 Evaluate an AI-in-enterprise proposal for value, feasibility, risk, and governance.
 Design a simple “enterprise AI feature” blueprint (e.g., enterprise search/RAG, ticket
triage, procurement assistant) with requirements, data needs, and controls.
 Communicate findings using clear stakeholder-facing artifacts: 1-page memo,
architecture diagram, risk register, KPI plan.

Weekly schedule (14 weeks, 3 credits)
Week 1 — What is an information system now?
IS in organizations; systems vs. workflows; digital + AI transformation overview.
Week 2 — Enterprise architecture basics
Strategy → business processes → apps → data/infrastructure (layered view).
Week 3 — Business processes + value
Process mapping (BPMN-lite), KPIs, bottlenecks, “where AI fits / doesn’t fit.”
Week 4 — Data foundations for IS
Master data, transactional vs. analytical systems, data quality, lifecycle.
Week 5 — Enterprise systems landscape
ERP/CRM/SCM, integration patterns, APIs, eventing (conceptual, not vendor-specific).
Week 6 — Cloud &amp; platform thinking for enterprise IS
Shared responsibility, scalability, reliability, cost basics (FinOps-lite).
Week 7 — Cybersecurity, privacy, compliance
Threat basics, least privilege, data classification, auditability—why enterprises care.
Week 8 — GenAI in enterprise: capabilities &amp; limits
Prompting as interface, summarization/classification, copilots, and failure modes.
Week 9 — Enterprise GenAI patterns
RAG/enterprise search, knowledge bases, tool-using agents (conceptual), human-in-the-loop
controls.
Week 10 — AI governance in the enterprise
Governance of data/models/systems; approvals, monitoring, accountability.
Week 11 — Build vs. buy; vendor and platform selection
Evaluation criteria, integration constraints, total cost, lock-in, procurement realities.
Week 12 — Change management + adoption
Operating model, training, rollout strategy, measurement, stakeholder management.
Week 13 — Responsible AI &amp; ethics in real deployments
Bias, transparency, safety, privacy; “responsible use” policies and controls.
Week 14 — Capstone presentations
Teams present an “Enterprise AI System Proposal” with architecture + governance + KPI plan.

Assignments &amp; grading (industry-aligned)
Weekly labs / applied exercises (20%): process map, data-flow diagram, prompt test plan, risk
checklist, KPI design
Case memos (20%): 2 short memos critiquing real enterprise AI/IS scenarios (HBS-style)
Midterm (20%): concepts + architecture + governance
Team capstone (40%): “Enterprise AI implementation proposal”
 Requirements + stakeholder map
 Architecture diagram (systems + data + AI component)
 Governance &amp; risk register (privacy/security/model risk)
 Rollout + change plan
 KPI/ROI measurement plan
Tooling (kept lightweight for an intro course)
 Diagramming (Lucidchart / draw.io)
 Spreadsheet-based analysis for KPIs
 A controlled GenAI sandbox for assignments
