# Why Now

The question isn't whether AI will transform professional work. That's already happening. The question is whether higher education will prepare students for the transformation — or graduate them into obsolescence.

The evidence says **now**. Not "soon." Not "eventually." Now.

---

## The Acceleration Evidence

### METR Task Completion Benchmarks

The Model Evaluation & Threat Research (METR) organization tracks the most rigorous measure of AI autonomous capability: the "time horizon" — the human-equivalent task duration at which AI agents succeed with a given reliability.

| Period | Doubling Time | What This Means |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| 2019–March 2025 | ~196 days (~7 months) | AI capability doubles roughly every 7 months |
| Since 2023 | 130.8 days (~4.3 months) | Acceleration: doubling time is shrinking |
| Since 2024 | 88.6 days (~2.9 months) | Further acceleration: capability doubles every ~3 months |

**Source:** [METR Time Horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/), updated February 20, 2026. Published in [arXiv:2503.14499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499).

The doubling time itself is decreasing — this is not linear growth or even simple exponential growth. It is **accelerating exponential growth**. The capability that takes AI three months to gain today took seven months two years ago.

### What This Means for a Four-Year Degree

A student entering the BS in Enterprise AI in Fall 2026 will graduate in Spring 2030. In that time:

- AI capability will double approximately **16 times** at current rates
- Tasks that take a human expert 1 hour today will be within AI reach
- The specific tools, frameworks, and APIs students learn in Year 1 will be obsolete by Year 3

This is why the program teaches **permanent human capabilities** — not transient technical skills. The 40/60 split, the Human Edge framework, and the Context Pack artifact are all designed for a world where the tools change constantly but the human substrate remains.

## The Broadway Photo

In 1900, Fifth Avenue in New York City was filled with horses and a single automobile. By 1913 — just thirteen years later — the same street was filled with automobiles and a single horse.

![Broadway 1900 vs. 1913](/bseai_degree_planning/horses_cars.jpg)

The people who bet on better horseshoes lost everything. The people who bet on understanding transportation — movement, logistics, human needs — thrived regardless of propulsion method.

We are in the same transition. Betting on better prompts, better tools, or better frameworks is betting on better horseshoes. Betting on judgment, inquiry, translation, resilience, and accountability is betting on understanding transportation.

### Bureau of Labor Statistics Data (2024–2034 Projections)

The BLS does not track "AI engineer" as a distinct occupation. Roles commonly labeled AI/ML engineer fall under these categories:

| Occupation | 2024 Median Salary | Projected Growth (2024–2034) | New Jobs |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------------|----------|
| Software Developers | $133,080 | +15% | ~287,900 |
| Data Scientists | $112,590 | +34% | Fastest-growing |
| Computer & Information Research Scientists | $140,910 | +20% | — |

**Source:** [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/), latest wages from May 2024 OEWS survey, projections release August 28, 2025.

### What BLS Doesn't Capture

BLS projections are conservative by design. They don't account for:

- **New role categories** that don't exist yet (AI systems orchestrator, human-AI workflow designer, evaluation engineer)
- **Salary premiums** for AI-specific skills — industry data from Levels.fyi and Glassdoor shows AI/ML engineers earning $150K–$350K at major tech companies
- **Cross-industry demand** — every industry (healthcare, finance, legal, manufacturing, government) is creating AI integration roles
- **The judgment premium** — as AI handles routine coding, the premium shifts to people who can evaluate, direct, and take accountability for AI systems

## The Higher Education Landscape

### What Other Institutions Are Doing

The higher education response to AI is rapidly evolving:

- **AI fluency requirements** are being added to general education across institutions — but most focus on *using* AI tools, not *building* AI systems or *judging* AI output
- **Assessment is shifting** from product to process: portfolios, oral defenses, audit logs, and version histories replace traditional exams
- **Capstone experiences** with authentic data and real stakeholders are becoming standard for AI-focused programs
- **The gap**: most programs either teach AI theory (CS/ML track) or AI tool use (business/analytics track). Very few teach the **integration layer** — building production AI systems with human judgment, evaluation, and accountability

### Where the BS in Enterprise AI Fits

| Approach | What It Teaches | What's Missing |
|----------|----------------|---------------|
| CS/ML programs | Algorithms, math, model training | Production systems, human judgment, client delivery |
| Business analytics | Data visualization, statistical tools | Engineering depth, system architecture, evaluation |
| Data science programs | Statistical modeling, Python/R, visualization | Infrastructure, deployment, knowledge engineering |
| **BS in Enterprise AI** | Everything above + integration, judgment, evaluation, accountability | — |

The BS in Enterprise AI occupies a unique position: it's the only program we've found that explicitly teaches the **human capabilities** that make AI professionals effective, not just the technical skills that AI is rapidly automating.

## The Proof of Concept

On a single evening in 2026, one person (a professor developing this program) simultaneously:

1. Built a friend's dog-care business: website, business plan, financials, operating manual, automation requirements
2. Worked on updating the BS in Enterprise AI curriculum
3. Updated a client's production website

This wasn't possible two years ago. It's possible now because AI handles the routine production while the human provides judgment, direction, and accountability across multiple parallel workstreams.

This is what our graduates will do daily — not write code, but **direct AI systems** while providing the human judgment that makes the output trustworthy. The program must teach them how.

## The Window

Every transition has a window — a period where institutions can position themselves or be positioned. The horse-to-car transition's window was roughly 1905–1915. The internet transition's window was roughly 1995–2005. 

The AI transition's window is **now through approximately 2028**. METR's doubling times mean the capability frontier moves faster than curriculum committee cycles. Programs that wait for "more evidence" will launch after the window closes.

The BS in Enterprise AI is designed to launch in Fall 2026, with full enrollment by Fall 2027 — squarely in the window.
