# The 40/60 Policy

> *40% of every studio course is done the hard way — building foundational understanding. 60% is done with AI agents — practicing the actual workflow graduates will use for the rest of their careers.*

---

## The Policy

Every studio course in the BS in Enterprise AI spine follows a standard split:

- **40% Hard-Way Work** — Students perform foundational tasks manually to understand *what the machine is doing.* This builds the substrate for informed judgment.
- **60% Agentic Work** — Students direct AI agents to build real systems, practicing context construction, output evaluation, iteration, and accountability.

This is not a suggestion or guideline. It is a **program-wide policy** that applies to all eight studio courses.

---

## Why 40%: The Judgment Substrate

You cannot exercise professional judgment over something you don't understand.

A student who has never written a SQL query cannot evaluate whether an AI-generated query is correct, efficient, or dangerous. A student who has never configured a firewall rule cannot assess whether an AI's security recommendation is appropriate. A student who has never structured an HTML document cannot judge whether an AI-generated layout meets accessibility standards.

The 40% hard-way work is not nostalgia for a pre-AI world. It is the *minimum investment* required to produce informed judgment. It builds:

- **Mental models** of how systems actually work (not how they appear to work)
- **Pattern recognition** for when output looks wrong
- **Debugging skill** for when the agent's work fails
- **Credibility** to evaluate AI in professional contexts

### Example: IS 117 (40% Hard-Way)
- Manually create HTML/CSS files from scratch (no AI)
- Navigate the filesystem with terminal commands
- Execute Git operations from the command line
- Debug layout issues by reading source code

After this, students *understand* what the AI is doing when they direct it in the 60% agentic portion.

---

## Why 60%: The Actual Workflow

The 60% agentic portion is not "use AI to make things easier." It is training in the **actual professional workflow** that employers are already paying $110K–$350K+ for.

What students practice in the 60%:

### Context Construction
Building the Context Pack — the brief that tells the AI agent what to do, what constraints to respect, and how to evaluate its own work. This is the meta-skill of the AI era.

### Output Evaluation
Examining what the AI produced and making informed decisions: accept, reject, or modify. Documenting the reasoning in the AI Audit Log.

### Iteration Management
When the first output isn't right, directing the AI toward improvement — not by guessing, but by applying the domain understanding built in the 40% to provide precise feedback.

### Multi-Agent Orchestration
By Year 3, students manage multiple agents across a project — one for code, one for testing, one for documentation — coordinating them as a "CEO of agents."

### Accountability
Taking responsibility for the final product regardless of who (or what) produced the components. The human's name goes on the work. The human presents at Demo Day.

## How It Works in Practice

### A Typical IS 218 Lab Session (2 hours)

**First 45 minutes (hard-way, ~40%):**
- Students write a test suite manually for a specific feature
- Run tests, see failures, debug by reading code
- Understand exactly what "passing" means for this component

**Next 75 minutes (agentic, ~60%):**
- Students construct a Context Pack: feature spec, test requirements, existing codebase context, evaluation criteria
- Direct AI agent to implement the feature
- Evaluate AI output against the test suite they wrote
- Document in AI Audit Log: what was accepted, what was modified, what was rejected and why
- Iterate until the feature passes all tests and meets quality standards

**Critical insight:** The hard-way portion *gives meaning to* the agentic portion. Students aren't just accepting AI output blindly. They're evaluating it against understanding they built minutes earlier.

## The Ratio Will Change — By Design

If METR's doubling time holds (∼3 months and accelerating), the floor of what AI handles autonomously will rise continuously. The 40/60 split defined in 2026 may need to become 30/70 by 2028 and 20/80 by 2030.

**This is expected and desirable.** As AI becomes more capable, students need less procedural practice and more evaluation/judgment practice. The policy includes a review mechanism:

- **Every semester:** Instructors assess whether the 40% hard-way content still represents the right foundational skills
- **Annual review:** Faculty committee evaluates whether the ratio should shift, based on current AI capabilities and employer feedback
- **Student feedback:** What did the hard-way work teach you that you couldn't have learned from the agentic work alone?

The principle is permanent: **enough manual work to build informed judgment, then practice the real workflow.** The ratio is a dial, not a law.

## Connection to the Stripping Thesis

The 40/60 split is the operational expression of the [Stripping Thesis](/bseai_degree_planning/philosophy/stripping-thesis/):

- The **40% hard-way** represents the procedural skills that AI will eventually automate completely — but that students must understand *now* to exercise judgment
- The **60% agentic** represents the permanently human work: directing, evaluating, deciding, taking responsibility

As the stripping continues, the 40% shrinks and the 60% grows. The human work remains. That's the point.
