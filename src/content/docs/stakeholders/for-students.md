# For Students

You're considering a degree that didn't exist two years ago, for jobs that didn't exist five years ago, using tools that are changing every month. Here's what that means and why it's the best investment you can make.

---

## What You'll Learn

The BS in Enterprise AI teaches you to **build, evaluate, and take accountability for AI-powered systems**. Not just use AI. Not just code. The integration of both — directing AI while providing the judgment that makes the output trustworthy.

### The Eight Studios

| Studio | Course | What You Build |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| 1 | IS 117 — Inquiry & Web Craft | Portfolio website + museum exhibit site |
| 2 | IS 118 — Interactive Computing | Interactive web app with API integration |
| 3 | IS 218 — Software Engineering | Full-stack web application, tested and deployed |
| 4 | IS 265 — Problems & Persuasion | Problem framing package for a real organization |
| 5 | IS 219 — Systems & Resilience | Containerized, observable, resilient service |
| 6 | IS 331 — Data & Knowledge | Hybrid retrieval system (SQL + graph + vector) |
| 7 | IS 390 — Systems Architecture | Complete systems analysis for an AI system |
| 8 | IS 425 — Applied AI Systems | Production AI system for a real client + Demo Day |

By graduation you'll have **8 deployed projects**, a professional portfolio, and Demo Day experience presenting to industry judges.

---

## What Makes This Different

### You Build Things — Every Semester

This is not a lecture-and-exam program. Every course is a studio: small groups, hands-on projects, real deliverables. You ship working systems, not just write papers about them.

### AI Is a Tool, Not a Crutch

You'll use AI coding assistants (Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude) from day one. But 40% of your work is done the hard way — manually — so you understand what the AI is doing. The other 60% is AI-directed: you're the pilot, AI is the co-pilot.

This is exactly how professionals use AI. The degree teaches you to do it that way from the start.

### The Human Edge

Every course develops a capability that AI cannot replicate:

| Capability | Why AI Can't Do This |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Disciplined Inquiry** | AI answers questions. It can't tell you if you're asking the right one. |
| **Translation** | AI generates interfaces. It can't decide what another person needs to understand. |
| **Professional Judgment** | AI generates code. It can't decide whether to ship it. |
| **Problem Finding** | AI optimizes any function. It can't tell you if you're optimizing the wrong thing. |
| **Resilience Thinking** | AI monitors dashboards. It can't decide whether to wake the team at 3 AM. |
| **Epistemic Humility** | AI processes data. It can't recognize when the data itself is wrong. |
| **Systems Thinking** | AI models components. It can't see the whole system's emergent behavior. |
| **Accountable Leadership** | AI can't be sued, fired, shamed, or praised. You can. |

These capabilities are what make you **irreplaceable**.

## What You'll Experience

### Year 1 (IS 117 + IS 118)
You arrive knowing nothing about code. By the end of the year you've built and deployed a portfolio site, a museum website, and an interactive web application with API integration. You've learned Git, HTML/CSS, JavaScript, and how to ask structured questions that get AI to produce useful output.

### Year 2 (IS 218 + IS 265)
You build a full-stack web application with testing and CI/CD. You learn to evaluate AI-generated code — accepting, rejecting, modifying with documented reasoning. Then you shift to human problems: stakeholder interviews, process mapping, persuasion, and identifying the *real* problem.

### Year 3 (IS 219 + IS 331)
You learn infrastructure: Linux, Docker, observability, incident response. You participate in an incident drill where your professor breaks your system and you have to fix it under pressure. Then you move to data systems: SQL, knowledge graphs, vector embeddings, and hybrid retrieval.

### Year 4 (IS 390 + IS 425)
You design a complete AI system for IS 390, then build it for a real client in IS 425. Knowledge graphs, human-in-the-loop workflows, evaluation harnesses. **Demo Day**: you present to industry judges and defend every decision. You graduate ready to ship production AI.

## What You Need to Know

### Prerequisites
No coding experience required. Seriously. IS 117 starts from zero. You need a laptop and willingness to work.

### The Studio Format
Studios cap at 20–25 students. Class time is hands-on: guided practice, code reviews, critiques, presentations. This is not a large lecture hall.

### Cost
All tools are free tier or NJIT-provided. No expensive software subscriptions, textbooks are affordable, cloud credits are provided.

### Workload
This is a demanding program. You'll be writing code, building systems, presenting to peers, managing client relationships, and reflecting on your decisions. Expect 10–15 hours/week per studio course (including class time).

### AI Policy
You are **expected** to use AI — it's part of the professional workflow. But you must document how you use it (AI Audit Logs, AI Use Notes), and you must be able to explain and defend every decision. "The AI wrote it" is never acceptable.

## Common Questions

### "Do I need to know how to code?"
No. IS 117 starts from zero. By the end of year 1, you'll be comfortable with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Git, and the terminal.

### "Is this program just about AI?"
No. It's about building professional capabilities that make you effective regardless of what AI can do. The technical skills happen to be AI-focused because that's where the highest demand and highest salaries are. But the human capabilities — judgment, inquiry, resilience, accountability — apply to any career.

### "What if AI makes this degree obsolete?"
That's exactly what this degree is designed to prevent. We don't teach you specific tools (those change every month). We teach you to evaluate, direct, and take accountability for AI systems — capabilities that become MORE valuable as AI gets more powerful.

### "Can I do this part-time or online?"
The studio model is designed for face-to-face cohorts. Online variants are possible for some courses. Part-time is possible but extends the timeline. Talk to an advisor.

### "What's Demo Day?"
It's a live presentation at the end of most courses where you demonstrate your working project to peers, faculty, and (in IS 425) industry judges. It's like a tech startup pitch + engineering review. It's nerve-wracking and incredibly valuable.
