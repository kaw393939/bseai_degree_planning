# For Faculty

The BS in Enterprise AI is not a traditional lecture-based program. It's a studio model — small cohorts, project-based, artifact-driven, with AI integrated into every course. This page covers what faculty need to know about teaching in this environment.

---

## The Studio Model

### What Changes

| Traditional | Studio |
|------------|--------|
| Lecture → exam | Short instruction → guided practice → project → critique |
| Individual work | Collaborative + individual |
| End-of-term assessment | Continuous artifact development + Demo Day |
| Content coverage | Capability development + content coverage |
| One modality | Multi-modality by design (face-to-face, converged, online variants) |

### What Stays the Same

- You are the domain expert; AI assists but doesn't replace your expertise
- Academic freedom in delivery approach, examples, and emphasis
- NJIT policies, grading standards, and academic integrity expectations
- Your relationship with students as mentor and evaluator

### Section Size

Studios cap at **20–25 students** (comparable to upper-division lab sections). This is intentional: the studio model requires individual feedback, code review, and mentorship that large lectures cannot provide.

---

## The 40/60 Split

Every course follows the 40/60 principle:

- **40% hard-way**: Students do things manually — write code by hand, debug with raw tools, sketch diagrams on paper, negotiate with stakeholders face-to-face
- **60% agentic**: Students direct AI assistants to build at velocity — generating code, documentation, analysis — while evaluating and refining output

### Why This Matters for Teaching

You don't need to ban AI or pretend it doesn't exist. You also don't let students use AI as a crutch. Instead:

1. **Design the 40% carefully** — these are the activities where understanding forms. Students who skip this can't evaluate AI output effectively.
2. **Design the 60% as professional practice** — students should use AI the way a senior engineer uses it: directing, evaluating, and taking accountability.
3. **The AI Audit Log / Use Note is your assessment tool** — students document their AI decisions, giving you visibility into their judgment process.

### Practical Example (IS 218, week 7)

| Phase | Time | What Students Do |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Hard-way | 40 min | Write a test by hand → run it → watch it fail → implement the feature manually → watch it pass |
| Agentic | 60 min | Direct Copilot to build three more features using the same pattern. Audit each suggestion. Log decisions. |
| Debrief | 15 min | Class discussion: where was AI helpful? Where was it wrong? What would you NOT trust it with? |

## The Human Edge Capability

Each course foregrounds one Human Edge capability. This isn't an add-on — it's embedded in the course design:

| Your Course | Your Capability | Your Artifact |
|------------|----------------|---------------|
| IS 117 | Disciplined Inquiry | Context Pack v1 + Inquiry Journal |
| IS 118 | Translation | Translation Brief |
| IS 218 | Professional Judgment | AI Audit Log |
| IS 265 | Problem Finding | Assumptions Log + Issue Tree |
| IS 219 | Resilience Thinking | Failure Mode Analysis + Postmortem |
| IS 331 | Epistemic Humility | Data Assumptions Document |
| IS 390 | Systems Thinking | Systems Decomposition |
| IS 425 | Accountable Leadership | Leadership Reflection |

**What this means for you:** You assess both the technical deliverable AND the Human Edge artifact. The artifact shows you whether students have developed the underlying capability, not just produced the output.

---

## The Context Pack

Every course uses the Context Pack — a recurring artifact that teaches students to assemble the information AI needs to be effective. It evolves in complexity:

- **IS 117**: Project Brief + basic requirements (simple)
- **IS 218**: Architecture patterns + testing criteria (intermediate)
- **IS 331**: Data assumptions + epistemic limits (advanced)
- **IS 425**: Full production system documentation (capstone)

**Why you should care:** The Context Pack gives you a window into whether students understand the project deeply enough to direct AI effectively. A good Context Pack = a student who can articulate what they're building and why. A bad Context Pack = a student who's copying prompts without understanding.

## Assessment Approach

### No Traditional Exams (for Most Courses)

Assessment is artifact-based: deployed projects, Human Edge documents, Context Packs, Demo Day presentations. If you want to include timed technical assessments (live coding exercises, design challenges), that's your call — but they should test judgment and application, not memorization.

### Demo Day

Every course ends with some form of Demo Day:
- Students present their work to peers (and where possible, industry guests)
- They demonstrate the working artifact, explain their decisions, and respond to questions
- Scoring includes: functionality, communication clarity, honest self-assessment, and evidence of the Human Edge capability

### Rubrics

Standardized rubrics are provided for:
- Human Edge artifacts (4-point scale: Exemplary / Proficient / Developing / Beginning)
- Context Pack quality
- Technical deliverables

You can customize within the framework, but the core criteria remain consistent across sections for assessment and accreditation purposes.

## What You'll Need

### Technical Setup
- GitHub classroom or organization for student repositories
- AI coding assistant access (GitHub Copilot education or equivalent)
- Course-specific tools (all in the syllabus, all free tier)
- Canvas for LMS, grades, rubrics, and feedback

### Teaching Support
- Syllabus templates and weekly schedules are provided
- Assessment rubrics are standardized and ready to use
- Learning objectives are Bloom's-aligned and mapped to outcomes
- Cross-course coordination meetings (lightweight, term-start and mid-term)

### What's Not Expected
- You don't need to be an AI expert to teach IS 117 or IS 265
- You don't need to rebuild your course from scratch — the scaffolding is provided
- You don't need to police AI use — the 40/60 structure handles this pedagogically

## Common Questions

### "What if AI changes faster than my course?"

That's the point. The program teaches **permanent human capabilities** with **disposable technical skills**. Your course's technical content will evolve — the Human Edge capability won't. Update the tools; keep the framework.

### "How do I assess if students just used AI for everything?"

The 40% hard-way component is your assessment of understanding. The AI Audit Log / Use Note is your assessment of judgment. Demo Day is your assessment of depth — students who don't understand their own work fail the Q&A.

### "What if I don't agree with the 40/60 ratio?"

The ratio is a guideline, not a rigid rule. Some weeks will be more hard-way, some more agentic. The principle is: students must build understanding before they can direct AI effectively. If you have a pedagogical reason to adjust, document it and discuss at the cross-course coordination meeting.

### "How does this affect my academic freedom?"

You retain full freedom in delivery approach, examples, emphasis, and pacing. The constraints are: (1) the learning outcomes are standardized for accreditation, (2) the Human Edge artifact is required, (3) the Context Pack is required, (4) Demo Day is required. Within those bounds, you teach your way.
