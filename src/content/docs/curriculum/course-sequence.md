# The Complete Degree

The BS in Enterprise AI is built around three layers: a **studio spine** that develops human capabilities and engineering fluency every semester, a **technical foundation** that provides the math and CS substrate, and **applied AI + enterprise courses** that deepen specific competencies.

Total: approximately 120–127 credits (per NJIT requirements).

---

## Layer 1: The Studio Spine (24 credits)

One studio per semester, 8 semesters, cohort cap 20–25. Each course produces portfolio-grade artifacts and develops one [Human Edge capability](/bseai_degree_planning/curriculum/outcomes/).

| Semester | Course | Title | Human Edge |
|----------|--------|-------|------------|
| Fall 1 | IS 117 | Inquiry & Web Craft | Disciplined Inquiry |
| Spring 1 | IS 118 | Interactive Computing | Translation |
| Fall 2 | IS 218 | Software Engineering | Professional Judgment |
| Spring 2 | IS 265 | Problems & Persuasion | Problem Finding |
| Fall 3 | IS 219 | Systems & Resilience | Resilience Thinking |
| Spring 3 | IS 331 | Data & Knowledge | Epistemic Humility |
| Fall 4 | IS 390 | Systems Analysis & Design | Systems Thinking |
| Spring 4 | IS 425 | Applied AI Systems | Accountable Leadership |

## Layer 2: Technical Foundation (~18 credits)

The math, CS, and statistics courses that make the studio spine credible. Without these, students can't reason about algorithms, evaluate models, or understand the math behind embeddings.

| Course | Title | When | Why |
|--------|-------|------|-----|
| CS 100 | Introduction to Computer Science (Python) | Fall 1 | Programming foundations; Python is the language of AI tooling |
| MATH 111 | Calculus I | Fall 1 | Required for stats and linear algebra; mathematical reasoning |
| DS 100 | Probability & Statistics | Spring 1 | Foundation for evaluation metrics, A/B testing, uncertainty |
| CS 114 | Introduction to Data Structures | Spring 1 | Algorithmic thinking; prereq for advanced CS |
| MATH 211 or DS 110 | Linear Algebra | Fall 2 | Required for understanding embeddings, transformations, ML math |
| MATH 226 | Discrete Analysis | Fall 2 or Spring 2 | Logic, proof, combinatorics — the math of computing |

**Design note:** These are standard NJIT offerings. The key decisions are:
- Python-first in CS 100 (not Java) — aligns with AI tooling ecosystem
- Linear algebra is non-negotiable — you cannot understand embeddings, transformations, or ML without it
- Statistics is early (Spring 1) — students need probabilistic thinking before they encounter evaluation metrics

## Layer 3: Applied AI & Domain Courses (~15 credits)

These courses deepen specific AI and enterprise competencies beyond what the spine covers.

| Course | Title | When | Purpose |
|--------|-------|------|---------|
| IS 350 | Computers, Society & Ethics | Fall 2 | The dedicated ethics course. AI regulation, bias auditing, system cards, privacy, societal impact. IS 390 handles systems analysis; IS 350 handles the "should we?" questions. Taken early so ethical reasoning is established before students build AI systems. |
| IS 247 | Designing the User Experience | Spring 2 | Human-centered design thinking. AI products that humans can't use are worthless. Taken alongside IS 265 to reinforce problem-finding with design methodology. |
| IS 392 | Applied AI & Language Systems | Fall 3 | NLP, prompt engineering, RAG architecture, chunking, embeddings, reranking. The technical depth needed for IS 390 and IS 425. |
| IS 465 | Machine Learning for Enterprise AI | Spring 3 or Fall 4 | Practical ML: problem framing (classification/regression/ranking), baselines, evaluation discipline, overfitting, interpretability. Not theory-heavy — focused on "when to use what and how to know if it's working." |
| IS 480 | AI Quality & Reliability Engineering | Fall 4 | Evaluation harnesses, red teaming, regression testing, hallucination measurement, online/offline eval, cost/quality tradeoffs. The QA course for AI systems. |

## Prerequisite Chain (Studio Spine)

```
IS 117 → IS 118 → IS 218 → IS 265
                                  ↓
                   IS 219 → IS 331 → IS 390 → IS 425
```

Each studio requires the prior one. No skipping. The accumulation model only works if every student goes through the full progression.

---

## What This Produces

A graduate of this program has:

- **8 portfolio projects** of increasing sophistication (one per semester)
- **8 Human Edge artifacts** demonstrating irreplaceable capabilities
- **Technical fluency** in Linux, Git, web development, databases, cloud infrastructure, and AI systems
- **AI-specific depth** in RAG, agents, evaluation, ethics, and governance
- **Professional experience** from the Service Learning Clinic and Industry Practicum
- **A Demo Day track record** — multiple presentations to industry judges, scored on evidence

This is not a list of courses taken. It is a body of evidence that a student can think, build, evaluate, lead, and take responsibility.
