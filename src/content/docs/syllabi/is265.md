## Course Description

This studio marks a critical pivot in the program. After three courses focused on building (pages, interactivity, full-stack applications), students now ask: **"Are we building the right thing?"**

The defining question: **"What's the real problem?"**

Students learn to navigate messy human systems — organizations with contradictory stakeholders, unstated requirements, political dynamics, and problems disguised as solutions. They practice stakeholder interviews, process mapping, requirements elicitation, and persuasion.

This is where students discover that "we need a chatbot" is never the real problem. The real problem is buried three layers deep, tangled in organizational politics, and nobody wants to say it out loud.

---

## Why Problem Finding?

AI can optimize any objective function you give it. It cannot tell you that you're optimizing the wrong thing.

Problem Finding requires navigating human contradiction — the stated requirement vs. the actual need, the official process vs. the real workflow, the meeting-room answer vs. the hallway truth. It requires reading between the lines, mapping political context, and distinguishing symptoms from root causes. No amount of intelligence — artificial or otherwise — can replace the human capacity to sit in a room with frustrated stakeholders and figure out what's actually going on.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Identify** the real problem in an organizational context, distinguishing symptoms from root causes (Analyze)
2. **Conduct** stakeholder analysis, mapping interests, power dynamics, and contradictions (Analyze)
3. **Model** business processes using standard notation and workflow diagrams (Apply)
4. **Elicit** requirements through interviews, observation, and document analysis (Apply)
5. **Construct** a problem framing memo with constraints, assumptions, and success metrics (Create)
6. **Present** technical recommendations to skeptical stakeholders using evidence and persuasion (Create)
7. **Construct** organizational Context Packs that include human dynamics and political context (Create)
8. **Apply** persuasion frameworks (Cialdini's principles, evidence-based argumentation) to stakeholder communication (Apply)
9. **Maintain** an assumptions log tracking what was assumed, investigated, and changed (Evaluate)

## Key Deliverables

### Problem Framing Package (25%)
A comprehensive document that a real organization could act on:
- Problem statement (the *real* problem, not the stated one)
- Stakeholder map with interests, power dynamics, and contradictions
- Current-state process model with pain points identified
- Constraints, assumptions, and success metrics
- Recommended approach with evidence
- Workflow prototype (wireframe + minimal implementation)

### Assumptions Log + Issue Tree — Human Edge Artifact (15%)
A living document maintained throughout the semester:
- Every assumption made, when it was investigated, and what changed
- Issue trees decomposing complex problems into testable sub-questions
- Evidence trail: what was validated, what was invalidated, what remains uncertain

This is the Problem Finding artifact — evidence that the student can navigate ambiguity systematically rather than jumping to solutions.

### Persuasion Presentation (15%)
Students present technical recommendations to a live panel role-playing skeptical stakeholders. Scored on:
- Accuracy of problem identification
- Quality of evidence
- Persuasion technique (Cialdini principles applied thoughtfully)
- Response to pushback
- Honesty about limitations

## The 40/60 Split in Practice

| Component | Hard-Way (40%) | Agentic (60%) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Interviews | Conduct real interviews; take notes by hand; read body language | AI-assisted transcription; theme identification; pattern analysis |
| Process mapping | Hand-draw current-state maps; walk the actual workflow | AI-assisted process documentation; automated bottleneck analysis |
| Requirements | Negotiate requirements with stakeholders face-to-face | AI-assisted requirements organization; traceability matrix generation |
| Prototyping | Sketch wireframes by hand; Paper prototyping | AI-directed prototype generation; rapid iteration on designs |

## Why This Course Comes After IS 218

> *"A 19-year-old who has never shipped software cannot do useful requirements analysis."*

Students who have built, tested, deployed, and maintained a real application can now look at a business problem and say: "I know what's possible and what's hard." They have intuitions about scope, complexity, and feasibility that make stakeholder conversations productive rather than abstract.
