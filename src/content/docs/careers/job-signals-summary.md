# Job Signals Summary

The AI labor market in 2025–2026 is sending a clear signal: **the jobs that are growing require deployment, evaluation, and judgment — not just coding.**

This page synthesizes what hiring signals mean for curriculum design.

---

## The Paradox: Wrong Jobs Dying, Right Jobs Growing

The headlines say "tech is laying off." The data says something more specific:

| Signal | Direction | Source |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Traditional programmer job postings | **Down ~51%** from 2021 peak | Indeed, 2025 |
| Computer programmer employment | **Down 25%+** (BLS projects continued decline) | BLS OOH, 2024 |
| AI-specific job postings | **Up 68%** YoY | Indeed AI Jobs Index, Feb 2026 |
| Software developer employment (BLS) | **+15.8%** projected growth (2024–2034) | BLS OOH, May 2024 |
| AI private investment (2025) | **$225.8 billion** | Stanford HAI AI Index, Apr 2025 |

The market is not shrinking. It is **reorganizing.** Routine coding is being automated. Enterprise AI deployment, evaluation, and governance roles are expanding.

---

## The Capital Demanding People

The investment driving this reorganization is unprecedented:

- **Big 4 hyperscaler capex (2025):** $300B+ combined (Microsoft $80B, Google $75B, Amazon $100B+, Meta $60–65B)
- **2026 capex projections:** $400B+ combined — an additional $100B+ over 2025
- **Total AI private investment (2025):** $225.8B (Stanford HAI)

Every dollar of infrastructure investment creates demand for people who can build, deploy, evaluate, and govern the systems that run on it. The infrastructure is being built. The question is whether there are enough trained people to use it.

---

## The Deployment Gap

Only **48% of AI projects reach production** (Gartner, 2024). The gap between prototype and production is where most AI investment fails to generate returns.

This is not primarily a model quality problem — frontier models are increasingly capable. It is a **people problem:**

- Who evaluates whether the AI output is correct?
- Who designs the deployment pipeline with rollback capability?
- Who communicates risks and limitations to stakeholders?
- Who takes accountability when the system fails?

These are Human Edge capabilities, not model parameters.

## Skills That Repeat Across These Roles

Every role above requires the same five skill clusters:

1. **Cross-functional delivery** — work with PM/design/research; write clear handoffs and technical docs
2. **LLM application engineering** — build LLM-backed features; design prompts and tool-use workflows
3. **Evaluation and AI QA** — build internal evals; iterate to improve quality and reduce risk
4. **Production readiness** — deploy to real systems; care about reliability, performance, and costs
5. **Modern software engineering** — strong Python + API skills; pragmatic engineering discipline

These map directly to the studio spine's technical coverage. See the [Skills Map](/bseai_degree_planning/careers/skills-map/) for the full capability-to-skill mapping.

---

## Curriculum Implications

The job signals point to three non-negotiable design decisions:

1. **Teach prototype → production explicitly** — not just notebooks. Every studio produces a deployed, running artifact.
2. **Make evaluation and AI QA a first-class outcome** — datasets, metrics, regression tests, eval harnesses. This is what separates "used ChatGPT" from "built a production AI system."
3. **Ensure students can ship LLM-backed workflows with guardrails and documentation** — the artifact isn't the code; it's the code + tests + docs + eval results + runbook.
